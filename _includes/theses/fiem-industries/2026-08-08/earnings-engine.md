## 2. Analysis Snapshot

### The business in one sentence

Fiem supplies lighting, signalling equipment, rear-view mirrors and related components, mainly to Indian two-wheeler OEMs; its earnings depend on OEM production, Fiem's content per vehicle, customer/model wins, realisations and manufacturing utilisation.

### What is improving

- **Fact:** Consolidated sales increased from ₹1,207.6 crore in FY2021 to ₹2,792.1 crore in FY2026, an 18.2% CAGR.
- **Fact:** EBITDA rose faster, from ₹133.8 crore to ₹395.9 crore; margin improved from 11.1% to 14.2%.
- **Fact:** PAT increased to ₹255.6 crore and ROCE reached approximately 28% in FY2026.
- **Fact:** The company has no conventional borrowings and held ₹279.4 crore cash at year-end.
- **Fact:** SIAM's Q1 FY2027 data shows a strong demand backdrop for both two-wheelers and passenger vehicles.
- **Management:** Most new lighting business is LED, and LED content can be three to four times conventional lighting value, depending on specification.
- **Management:** FY2027 four-wheeler revenue is expected at ₹100–150 crore, rising to ₹200–250 crore in FY2028.

### What prevents a high-conviction conclusion

- FY2026 trade receivables rose approximately 60%, weakening CFO/PAT to 63%.
- TVS and Honda together represented approximately 57.5% of FY2026 automotive revenue; the top five named OEM groups represented about 86.7%.
- Four-wheeler RFQs in development are not firm orders. Management explicitly corrected the interpretation that 70% movement to development equals an order.
- EBITDA margin is already at a record level, while management guides to “around 14%,” limiting automatic margin-expansion assumptions.
- At ₹2,582 the stock trades at 26.6× FY2026 EPS and about 16.6× trailing EBITDA on this calculation.

### Core model question

Can Fiem sustain 14% EBITDA margins while combining two-wheeler demand, higher LED content and four-wheeler scale into 12–16% cash-backed earnings growth?

## 3. Business Engine

### Economic chain

`OEM vehicle production × Fiem fitment share × lighting/mirror content per vehicle × realisation = automotive revenue`

`Automotive revenue + small non-automotive revenue − materials − employees − other operating costs = EBITDA`

`EBITDA − depreciation + other income − finance cost − tax = PAT`

`PAT + non-cash charges − working-capital investment − capex = approximate free cash flow`

### Product and customer economics

- **Automotive lighting:** approximately 75% of FY2026 automotive revenue when LED and conventional lighting are combined. This is the primary value pool.
- **LED:** 47.5% of automotive revenue was classified as automotive LED lighting; management also described LED as about 63% of automotive-lighting revenue. Both can be true because their denominators differ.
- **Rear-view mirrors:** 10.7% of automotive revenue.
- **Domestic OEM:** 93.2% of automotive revenue; replacement 5.0%; exports 1.9%.
- **Customer concentration:** TVS 32.7%, Honda 24.8%, Yamaha 14.2%, Suzuki 9.3%, Royal Enfield 5.8% of FY2026 automotive revenue.

The moat is engineering integration, qualification, proximity and execution. It is valuable but not invulnerable: pricing remains negotiated with large OEM customers, and platform losses or customer underperformance can affect revenue quickly.

### What history says about the model

1. **Operating leverage exists.** FY2021–FY2026 sales compounded at 18.2%, EBITDA at 24.1%, and PAT faster still.
2. **The current margin is not a trough.** FY2026's 14.2% is the highest in the six-year series. The base model therefore holds margin near 14.1% instead of extrapolating expansion.
3. **Growth can slow sharply.** FY2024 sales grew only 9.9%, showing that a high-teens trajectory is not automatic.
4. **Capital returns improved with scale.** ROCE increased from 11.7% to 28.0%, supporting the view that existing capacity and integrated manufacturing create leverage.
5. **Cash conversion is the present exception.** FY2026 profit quality was materially weaker than the P&L trend.

### Hard-period calibration

FY2021 had an 11.1% EBITDA margin and 3.9% PAT margin. Fiem is now structurally larger, less indebted and more LED-oriented, so returning fully to FY2021 profitability is not the base risk case. The bear case uses a 13.2% FY2027 margin—below recent performance but above the older trough.

## 11. Retail Explanation

### How to think about Fiem

Fiem is like a supplier whose income rises when its motorcycle and scooter customers make more vehicles, when Fiem wins a place on more models, and when each vehicle uses more valuable lighting. LED lighting can increase the value of components on a vehicle, but it does not automatically give Fiem a higher profit percentage.

The passenger-car opportunity is promising but small today. An RFQ is similar to being invited into a serious discussion and development process; it is not the same as receiving a guaranteed purchase order. Investors should count revenue only when production begins and Fiem invoices the customer.

### What the receivable increase means

Trade receivables increased from about **₹242 crore to ₹388 crore**. These are sales Fiem recorded for which customers had not yet paid cash at year-end.

In everyday language:

> Fiem sold more products and booked the sales as revenue, but much more of the money was still sitting with its customers instead of in Fiem's bank account.

The nearly **₹149 crore receivable working-capital outflow** does not mean the money was necessarily lost. It means approximately that much additional cash became tied up in unpaid invoices during FY2026. If customers pay normally, that cash can appear later. If debtor days keep rising, however, reported profit becomes less useful because the business must finance its customers for longer.

Management says the jump occurred because Fiem stopped asking banks to pay certain invoices early through bill discounting. That is plausible and may save financing cost, but investors should verify it through subsequent cash collection rather than accepting the explanation on faith.

### The retail takeaway

- **Business direction:** positive.
- **Balance sheet:** strong.
- **Earnings quality in FY2026:** weaker than the PAT headline.
- **Share-price expectation:** already reasonably high.
- **What matters next:** cash collection, 14% margin and actual four-wheeler invoices.

## 12. Final Verdict

### Direct answers required by the Earnings Engine

1. **Underlying business direction:** Positive. Sales, EBITDA, PAT and ROCE have all improved over six years, and Q1 FY2027 industry data provides a supportive demand backdrop.
2. **Most important operating driver:** Two-wheeler OEM revenue, particularly TVS/Honda volumes and Fiem's content per vehicle. Four-wheeler revenue is the most important incremental driver, but not yet the largest.
3. **Model versus market expectations:** The base FY2027 model produces ₹111 EPS and a ₹2,668 value at 24×, close to the supplied ₹2,582 price. The market is already pricing a substantial portion of the base case.
4. **What the bull case requires:** high-teens two-wheeler revenue growth, ₹150 crore four-wheeler revenue, 14.6% EBITDA margin and visible receivable normalisation.
5. **What invalidates the base case:** FY2027 revenue below industry growth together with EBITDA margin below 13.5%, or receivables continuing to outgrow sales so CFO/PAT remains below 70%.
6. **Top three quarterly metrics:** revenue growth versus underlying OEM/industry volumes; EBITDA margin; receivable days/CFO conversion.

### Classification

**Business quality:** Good and improving  
**Earnings visibility:** Medium  
**Cash-flow confidence:** Medium-Low pending FY2027 collection evidence  
**Valuation comfort at ₹2,582:** Low-Medium  
**Overall research stance:** Strong study candidate, but the current price offers limited base-case valuation cushion.

This is not a “bad company” conclusion. It is a distinction between a good operating business and an investment price that already expects good execution.
