## Thesis in one minute

Fiem Industries is a profitable, effectively debt-free automotive-component company with strong positions in two-wheeler lighting and mirrors. Rising LED content and an early four-wheeler business provide identifiable growth opportunities. The company has grown revenue, profit and return on capital strongly.

The main caution is that FY2026 profit did not convert into cash as well as before. Trade receivables rose much faster than sales, while the share price already assumes that Fiem can keep compounding earnings at a demanding rate.

> **Research view:** the business is moving in the right direction, but the valuation leaves limited room for weak execution. Cash collection, a roughly 14% EBITDA margin, and actual four-wheeler invoices matter more than announcements.

## What is attractive

- Long relationships with TVS, Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki, Royal Enfield and other OEMs.
- Integrated lighting, electronics, tooling, validation and manufacturing capabilities.
- Rising LED content can increase the value supplied per vehicle.
- No conventional debt and substantial cash provide room to fund planned investment.
- The new four-wheeler business can diversify revenue if development work becomes serial production.

## What can go wrong

- Two-wheeler and customer concentration remain high.
- Four-wheeler RFQs and development programmes are not guaranteed orders.
- A record margin can fall if raw-material pass-through, pricing or utilisation weakens.
- Receivables may remain high and prevent reported profit from becoming cash.
- Further promoter selling, factory incidents or delayed insurance settlements could weaken confidence.
- A high earnings multiple can contract even when the company remains profitable.

## The cash-flow issue, in plain language

Trade receivables are invoices that Fiem has recorded as sales but has not yet collected in cash.

Fiem's receivables increased from approximately **₹242 crore to ₹388 crore**. The cash-flow statement recorded a nearly **₹149 crore outflow** caused by receivables.

This does not necessarily mean ₹149 crore was lost. It means much more money was still with customers at year-end instead of in Fiem's bank account. Management says the increase mainly occurred because Fiem stopped using banks to receive certain invoice payments early. That explanation is plausible, but it needs to be verified through lower debtor days and stronger operating cash flow in FY2027.

## What growth is already priced in?

Using a ten-year horizon, a 12% required return and a 20× future P/E:

- current normalized PAT: approximately ₹256 crore;
- required FY2036 PAT: approximately ₹1,055 crore;
- required annual PAT growth: **15.2%**;
- required profit increase: more than four times.

At Fiem's current PAT margin, revenue would need to increase from approximately ₹2,792 crore to roughly ₹11,533 crore over ten years.

The existing two-year Earnings Engine base grows PAT at about 14.9% annually, close to the hurdle. The difficult part is maintaining comparable growth for most of a decade while improving cash conversion.

## What to check every quarter

1. Revenue growth relative to underlying two-wheeler and customer volumes.
2. EBITDA margin remaining near 14%.
3. Actual billed four-wheeler revenue rather than RFQ value.
4. Trade-receivable growth and debtor days.
5. Operating cash flow relative to PAT.
6. ROCE after new capex.
7. Promoter transactions, safety incidents and insurance settlements.

## Retail conclusion

Fiem appears to be a good operating business at a price that already expects good execution. The next evidence should come from collections, margins and invoiced four-wheeler sales—not only reported PAT or management commentary.
